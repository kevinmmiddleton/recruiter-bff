# Preferences Page: Template and How to Build It

`job-search/preferences.md` is the user's one-page answer to "what do you want next?" It's written to be shared: with former colleagues, recruiters, friends, anyone who might help. Pay, the skip list, and saved searches stay private in `profile.md`; this page has no numbers anyone would mind seeing.

Every skill reads it. Scans filter on the must-nots and the company preferences. The fit check reads a posting against it ("does this fit what you want?"). Interview guides turn must-haves and must-nots into questions to ask. Materials make the resume summary and LinkedIn headline say the one sentence. Contacts include the one sentence in every ask, so people know what to watch for.

The idea of a shareable preferences page, the one-sentence target, and the early-conversation questions are adapted from Phyl Terry's *Never Search Alone: The Job Seeker's Playbook*. The wording here is this plugin's own.

---

```markdown
# What I want next

## In one sentence

[Role] at [kind of company], [the condition that matters most].
If I can't get there in one move: [step one], then [step two].

## What I love doing

- ...

## What I'm good at but don't want to do all day

- ...

## What I'd hate doing

- ...
- Fine without: ...

## Must-haves

- ...

## Must-nots

- ...

## The company

- **Size:** under 50 / 50 to 250 / 250 to 1,000 / 1,000 to 10,000 / 10,000+
- **Stage and ownership:** early startup / growth stage / public / private equity owned / family owned / nonprofit / government
- **Subjects that draw me** (I'd care about this every day): ...
- **I'd consider:** ...
- **No thanks:** ...
- **Team and manager:** ...
- **What good culture looks like to me:** ...

## Logistics

- **Where:** city, remote, hybrid, in office; travel
- **Schedule needs outside work:** ...

## Career goals

- **Next:** (one to three options is fine)
- **Long run:** ...

## What I've heard from the market

- **Easy yes, stretch, out of reach** (from a recruiter): ...
- **What people who've worked with me keep saying:** ...
- **Where I keep stalling:** ...
```

## How to build it (the conversation)

Run this as a real conversation, a few questions at a time. It's the longest part of setup, so offer it after the first scan, never before. People who'd rather do it later can; nothing else waits on it.

1. **Start with what they'd hate.** Dislikes are easier to name than wants. "Think about your last few jobs. What drained you, and why?"
2. **The bad-boss question.** "Have you had a bad boss or a bad job? What did it teach you about what you don't want?" The real must-nots come from here.
3. **Flip them.** Turn each must-not into a must-have, with a number when there is one ("hate constant travel" becomes "travel at most once a month"). The opposite of a dislike isn't always a love. Sometimes it's just "fine without it," and the page has a line for that.
4. **Then what they love.** Ask separately what they're good at but don't enjoy. Those are different, and people drift into roles built on the second list.
5. **The company.** Offer the size bands and ownership types as choices. Then the subject question, kept apart from strategy: "Would you care about this subject every day?" is a different question from "Would it look good on your resume?" A role can score well and still be wrong if the subject bores them.
6. **Goals.** Next and long run. Several options are fine at the start.
7. **Draft the one sentence.** Concrete words only, words a recruiter could search for: "a senior product manager role on a growth team at a consumer subscription company, hybrid in New York." Abstract phrases ("a mission-driven, people-first culture") don't help anyone help you. If the goal isn't reachable in one move, write the two steps.
8. **Close with homework, not a lecture.** "This is a draft until three people who know your work have seen it. Pick three, send them the page, and ask four questions:"
   - If you were in my shoes, how would you approach this search?
   - Is this accurate?
   - Which goal fits me best?
   - Who else should I talk to?

   Offer to draft the message that goes with it. The user sends it.

## Keeping it current

- When the user reports what people said, update the page and the "What I've heard from the market" section, and change the "draft" line once three people have reacted.
- Preferences move as the market answers back. The weekly review asks once: "Is anything in your preferences changing?"
- If a fit check or a scan reveals a preference the page doesn't have ("I'd never work in ad tech"), offer to add it.

## Rules

- **The user's words.** Write what they said, tightened, not what sounds impressive.
- **No pay on this page.** Pay tiers live in `profile.md`.
- **Never gate anything on it.** Scans, fit checks, and guides work without it, and get better with it.
